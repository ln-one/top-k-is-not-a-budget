# The two original poison queries are not the same kind of failure

## Query 855410: “what is theraderm used for”

This is an early-saturation case. The exhaustive fused ranks 1–4 are the four
judged-positive passages, all directly about Theraderm skin-care products. Rank
5 is judged nonrelevant. Exact Top-5 certifies after balanced depth 8, and the
judged Recall@5 and nDCG@5 are both 1.0. Expanding from K=5 to K=10 retrieves no
additional judged-positive passage.

The certificate becomes stuck at fused positions 7–8:

| Role | External ID | Dense rank | Sparse rank | Known WRRF lower score | Judgment | Content |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Candidate | 3723411 | 6 | >5,000 | 0.015384615 | unjudged | Thermi-Gen radio-frequency skin procedure |
| Competitor | 1147809 | >5,000 | 6 | 0.015384615 | 0 | keyword-list page repeating “what is … used for” |

The documents are tied because each is rank 6 in one channel and unseen in the
other. At depth 5,000, either could still receive as much as 1/5,060 from its
missing channel. Exact ordering therefore requires resolving tiny tail
contributions for two documents that add no judged utility to this query. The
live K=20 EAHR run consequently had a 507.271 s median versus 19.813 s for the
exhaustive batch plan.

For an online variable-K system, this is the clean case: retain the already
certified Top-5 and decline expansion when adequacy is high, new candidates are
off-topic/redundant, and the first expansion exposes a cross-channel collision.

## Query 443396: “lps laws definition”

This is an ambiguity-and-coverage case. “LPS” has several plausible senses.
The fused Top-10 mixes the intended Lanterman–Petris–Short Act with limited
partnership law, lightning-protection systems, and LP-gas material. Only one
judged-positive document appears in Top-10. A second appears by K=20, a third by
K=50, and two more by K=100. Judged Recall therefore rises from 1/94 at K=10 to
5/94 at K=100, although nDCG declines as the cutoff grows.

Top-5 and Top-10 certify at balanced depths 129 and 697. K=20 becomes stuck at
fused positions 16–17:

| Role | External ID | Dense rank | Sparse rank | Known WRRF lower score | Judgment | Content |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Candidate | 2638083 | 3 | >5,000 | 0.016129032 | unjudged | legal-process outsourcing |
| Competitor | 8079046 | >5,000 | 3 | 0.016129032 | 0 | generic acronym-definition page for “LPS” |

Again, equal one-channel ranks create equal lower scores, and either document
may still obtain a small opposite-channel contribution. The blocking pair is
not responsible for the one extra relevant item recovered by K=20, but exact
ordered Top-20 cannot be returned without resolving it. The live K=20 EAHR run
had a 98.445 s median versus 20.971 s for exhaustive batch execution.

This case shows why “always stop when certification becomes expensive” is not a
complete policy. The observed results still look semantically unresolved, and
larger K does recover additional judged-positive documents. An online system
needs both an adequacy signal and a cost signal, followed by a declared fallback
when they disagree.

## Consequence for the next method

The two cases imply a sequential, anytime contract rather than a query-only K
predictor:

1. Certify a small prefix, such as K=5.
2. Estimate whether the current evidence is adequate and whether the next
   result block is likely to add utility.
3. Start expanding only when expected utility justifies it, reusing the current
   channel streams.
4. Monitor certificate growth and blocking-pair geometry during expansion.
5. If the cost budget is reached, return the largest already certified prefix,
   continue under an explicit larger budget, or invoke a separately declared
   relaxed fallback.

Query 855410 should normally terminate at its exact Top-5. Query 443396 may
justifiably request more evidence, but it also demonstrates that variable K
alone cannot remove every poison case: some useful expansions cross an
irrelevant ordering collision. The research problem is therefore online
utility–cost stopping with a safe fallback, not merely predicting K from the
query text.
