Hybrid retrieval for RAG often fuses fixed-depth dense and sparse rankings. Exact fusion avoids this truncation, but completing a fixed Top-$K$ can require sharply different numbers of accesses across queries. DiBud (Direct Budgeting) takes an access budget directly and returns the certified prefix available within it. Incremental certification determines the output size, while selective access resolves remaining ranking uncertainty. Replays on five query sets show that, at 2048 accesses, DiBud certifies 7.86\% more results within the first 100 positions than balanced access. After budget calibration for 95\% of exact Top-20 quality, held-out NFCorpus and SciFact queries retain 95.81\% and 97.68\% of nDCG@20 while reducing accesses by 65.92\% and 83.41\%. Five corpus snapshots and observation-limited cases expose variation in output size and the limits of this tradeoff.
