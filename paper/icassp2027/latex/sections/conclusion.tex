\section{Conclusion}
Fixed-depth fusion can change its relative effectiveness across queries and corpus snapshots, while exact Top-$K$ completion retains a variable access cost. DiBud directly bounds sorted accesses and lets incremental certification determine the returned prefix. Matched-budget replays show increased output over balanced access, and held-out budget calibration demonstrates substantial access savings at high mean quality retention on several query sets. The tradeoff varies across collections: fewer accesses can mean fewer returned results, and a quality target need not transfer to every query. The evidence concerns logical accesses to exact ranked lists; online retrieval latency remains outside this evaluation.
